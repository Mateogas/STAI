# Reading Your Meridian Labs Payslip

Your first payslip can be a shock: the number that lands in your bank account is
noticeably smaller than salary ÷ 12. That's normal, it's the same for everyone,
and this page explains every line. Payslips live in **Meridian People → Pay**.
Questions about a specific line: Anita Kapoor (Payroll Specialist) via
**#ask-people**.

## When you get paid

- Payroll runs **semi-monthly**: the **15th** and the **last business day** of
  each month (if the 15th is a weekend, you're paid the Friday before).
- Each paycheck covers half a month, so one paycheck ≈ annual salary ÷ 24.
- **Your first paycheck is usually prorated**: if you started mid-period, you're
  paid only for the days you actually worked in that period. It corrects itself
  from the next full period — nothing is missing.
- No direct deposit details on file = paper check = slow. See *Pre-Employment
  Requirements*.

## Gross vs. net — the one idea that explains everything

- **Gross pay** — your salary for the period, before anything is taken out.
- **Deductions** — everything subtracted (taxes, insurance, retirement).
- **Net pay** ("take-home") — what actually hits your bank account.

Typical net for a single filer with default choices is roughly **70–80% of
gross**. If yours is in that range, nothing is wrong.

## The deduction lines, one by one

1. **Income tax withholding** — an estimate of your income tax, sent to the
   government every paycheck on your behalf. The amount is driven by the tax
   election form you filed (see *Pre-Employment Requirements*). At year-end, if
   too much was withheld you get a refund when you file your tax return; too
   little and you owe the difference. Withholding is not an extra tax — it's
   prepayment of the tax you'd owe anyway.
2. **Social insurance contributions** — mandatory public programs (state pension
   and public medical insurance). A fixed percentage of gross; every employer in
   the country deducts these. They fund your future public pension and health
   entitlements.
3. **401(k) contribution** — your retirement savings (default 4%). This is *your
   money* going into *your account*, not a cost. The company match does NOT
   appear as a deduction — it's added on top, and you can see it in Meridian
   People → Benefits → Retirement. See *Benefits 101 Explainer*.
4. **Medical / dental / vision premium** — your share of insurance costs. On
   Aurora Base the medical line is $0.00; dental and vision are $8.00 each. These
   are **pre-tax**: they reduce the income the tax in line 1 is computed on.
5. **Commuter deduction** (only if you opted in) — pre-tax transit/parking, see
   *Office Logistics*.

## The additions you might see

- **Wellness stipend** — +$50/month, added automatically (see *Benefits
  Overview*). It is taxable, so it appears as income.
- **Profit-share** — February only, when company targets are met.
- **Expense reimbursements** — not salary; repayment of money you spent. Not taxed.

## Worked example (illustrative only)

Salary $70,000 → semi-monthly gross **$2,916.67**:

| Line | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Gross pay | $2,916.67 |
| Income tax withholding | −$370.00 |
| Social insurance | −$223.00 |
| 401(k) (4%) | −$116.67 |
| Dental + vision | −$16.00 |
| Wellness stipend | +$50.00 |
| **Net pay** | **$2,241.00** |

≈ 77% of gross — right in the normal range.

## When to actually worry (and ask Payroll)

- Your hours/salary line doesn't match your offer letter.
- A deduction appears that you never elected.
- Your net changes significantly with no election change (tax tables update each
  January — a small January change is normal).
- You were paid late. That is never normal — flag it immediately in #ask-people.
