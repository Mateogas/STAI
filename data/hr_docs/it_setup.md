# IT Setup Guide

Everything about your laptop, accounts, and security basics. IT is run by
**Tomas Lindgren (IT Support Lead)**; the help desk lives on **Floor 3** and in
Slack at **#it-help** (email: helpdesk@meridianlabs.io). Security questions go to
**Grace Obi (Security & Compliance Officer)**.

## Day one: laptop & accounts

1. **Laptop pickup** — Floor 3 IT desk, 11:00 on your first day (see *First Day
   Guide*). Engineers get a 16" MacBook Pro or ThinkPad X1 (your choice, asked in
   the pre-start survey); everyone else gets a 14" of the same options.
2. **First login** — temporary password from IT; you'll be forced to set a new
   one. Passphrases beat passwords: four random words is both stronger and easier.
3. **MFA (multi-factor authentication)** — mandatory on every company account.
   Install the authenticator app on your phone at the IT desk so you're never
   locked out. MFA is the single most effective thing you do for security.
4. **Password manager** — Meridian provides a 1Password business account.
   Non-negotiable for work credentials; the free family plan for personal use is
   a perk.
5. **Email & calendar** — firstname.lastname@meridianlabs.io on Google Workspace.
6. **Slack** — our chat. Join **#new-hires**, **#ask-people**, **#it-help**, your
   team channel, and anything that looks fun (#pets is the traditional first
   join).

## Week one

- **VPN** — required for internal systems from outside the office. Installer and
  guide: The Hub → IT → VPN. Test it from home in week one, not the night you
  first need it.
- **Security awareness training** — a 45-minute module in The Hub, due within
  your first 30 days (it's on your onboarding plan). Grace Obi runs a live
  Q&A version every second Tuesday if you prefer humans to videos.
- **Phone (optional)** — company Slack/email on your personal phone requires the
  device-management profile (it can wipe *company data only* — it cannot see
  your photos, messages, or location). Entirely optional; a company phone can be
  requested instead through your manager.

## Engineers: dev environment

Your development setup happens **with your onboarding buddy in week one** — not
alone on day one. The team's setup script and docs live in the platform repo
(your buddy will share access once your GitHub org invite lands, usually day 2).
Ship your first starter PR by end of week one — it's deliberately tiny; the
point is exercising the whole pipeline (clone → branch → PR → review → CI →
merge), not impressing anyone.

## Security basics (the short version)

The full policy is in the *Code of Conduct*; the daily-life version:

- **Lock your screen** when you stand up (Win+L / Ctrl-Cmd-Q). Yes, even for
  coffee. The office runs a friendly "unlocked laptop → you owe the team
  pastries" tradition — cheaper to just lock it.
- **Customer data stays in approved systems** — never in personal email, personal
  cloud drives, or pasted into unapproved AI tools (an approved internal
  assistant exists — you're probably talking to it).
- **Phishing** — when an email smells off (urgency + links + credentials), use
  the "Report phish" button in Gmail. False alarms are fine and expected.
- **USB sticks found in the wild** go to IT, not into your laptop. (Yes, it has
  happened.)
- **Lost or stolen device** — report within 4 hours to #it-help or the 24/7 line
  on the back of your badge. You will never be punished for reporting fast;
  delay is the only failure mode.
