# Benefits 101 — for people whose first job is this one

Nobody teaches this in school. This page explains what employee-benefit words
actually mean and what a sensible default choice looks like. It pairs with the
*Benefits Overview* (the official numbers) — read this page first, then that one.
There are no silly questions here; if anything is still unclear, ask this
assistant or Sofia Reyes (Benefits Coordinator).

## Health insurance words, decoded

- **Premium** — the monthly price of having the insurance at all. At Meridian,
  your share of the premium is taken out of your paycheck automatically
  (pre-tax, which means it also slightly lowers your income tax).
- **Deductible** — how much you pay out of your own pocket for care each year
  *before* insurance starts paying most of the bill. Low premium usually means
  high deductible, and vice versa. That's the entire trade-off.
- **Copay** — a small fixed fee (like $25) for a doctor visit or prescription,
  even after the deductible.
- **In-network** — doctors and hospitals your insurer has prices agreed with.
  Staying in-network is dramatically cheaper. Aurora Health's app shows who's
  in-network near you.

**A sensible default if this is your first job:** young, healthy, no regular
prescriptions → **Aurora Base** ($0/month) and put the money you saved into your
401(k). See a doctor regularly or take medication → **Aurora Plus**. You can
change plans every November, so no choice is forever.

## The 401(k), decoded (please read this one)

A **401(k)** is a retirement account you pay into straight from your paycheck,
before tax. Meridian **matches** what you put in, dollar for dollar, up to 4% of
your salary.

Read that again: contribute 4% and the company hands you another 4%. **It is an
instant, guaranteed 100% return.** Not enrolling at least to the match is
leaving salary unclaimed — it is the single most common first-job money mistake.

Worked example: salary $70,000. You contribute 4% = $2,800/year. Meridian adds
$2,800. Invested for ~40 years at average market growth, that one year's
combined $5,600 is on the order of $80,000 at retirement. Starting at 25 versus
35 roughly **doubles** where you end up, because the earliest money compounds
longest.

At Meridian you're auto-enrolled at 4% — the right move is usually to leave it
alone (or raise it). The match is vested immediately (yours even if you quit next
year — at many companies it isn't, so this is unusually good).

## Other words you'll meet

- **Pre-tax deduction** — money taken out of your pay *before* income tax is
  calculated, so it lowers your tax. 401(k), health premiums, commuter benefit.
- **Vesting** — when promised money actually becomes yours. (Meridian's match:
  immediately.)
- **Beneficiary** — who receives your life-insurance payout. Takes 2 minutes to
  set in Meridian People; do it in week one.
- **EAP** — free confidential counseling (stress, money, legal, family). Using
  it is invisible to your manager and HR. Details in *Benefits Overview*.
- **FSA/HSA-style accounts** — Meridian doesn't offer these currently, so you
  can skip learning them for now.

## Your week-one benefits checklist

1. Open Meridian People → Benefits.
2. Pick a medical plan (default suggestion above) + dental + vision.
3. Confirm 401(k) at 4% or more.
4. Name a life-insurance beneficiary.
5. Done — takes about 20 minutes total. Deadline: **30 days from your start
   date** (hard deadline — after that you wait for November open enrollment).

Your first payslip will show these choices as deductions — the *Payslip
Explainer* walks through exactly what each line means.
